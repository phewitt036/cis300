Why this exists

I built Bench Notes for my own fantasy league. The hard part turned out not to be writing the advisor — it was getting seven other people to trust it.

A tool that just says “start him” is easy to ignore. So this site explains what the advisor looks at, where its data comes from, and what it does not know.

How it was built

The data

League settings and rosters come from Yahoo's Fantasy API, through a developer app registered under the Bench Notes name. Projections and injury status come from Sleeper, and headlines from RotoWire and ESPN's public feeds.

The scoring

Written in Python. It re-scores every projection with our league's exact rules, has an AI model rate the news, and gives the start to the best score at each position. It runs on a schedule on a small server in my home.

This site

Hand-written HTML and one CSS file for CIS 300 at Arizona State University, with no frameworks or templates. It is the front door that explains the tool to people who will never read its code.
